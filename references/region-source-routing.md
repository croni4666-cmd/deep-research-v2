# Region-aware source routing

Use this reference when jurisdiction, geography, language, population, market,
or culture could materially change the evidence or conclusion.

## Route by claim type and provenance

Classify the research question before choosing sources:

- **law or policy:** governing text, regulator, legislature, court, official
  implementation guidance, then analysis;
- **medical or public health:** local guideline and regulator, local population
  data or registry, original studies, then systematic synthesis;
- **market or company:** statutory filing, company disclosure, official
  statistics, transaction or pricing data, then industry analysis;
- **academic or technical:** original paper, dataset, standard, specification,
  release record, then review or commentary;
- **social or cultural:** representative survey or administrative data for
  prevalence claims; community material only for bounded experience claims.

Search in the target context's working language and in an international
research language when useful. Use local terminology, agency names, document
identifiers, and date variants. Language is a discovery dimension, not an
authority score.

## Source roles

Keep locality and evidentiary role separate:

1. **Local primary:** governing or original evidence from the target context.
2. **External primary:** original evidence from another context or an
   international comparative source.
3. **High-quality synthesis:** a traceable review, analysis, or news account.
4. **Discovery or experience:** search indexes, community discussion, and other
   sources that identify leads or document bounded lived experience.

Do not label a source primary merely because it is local, official merely
because it uses a government-like domain, or independent merely because it is
hosted elsewhere. A community source can be primary evidence of what that
community member reported, but not of population prevalence or causality.

## Regional evidence note

For each load-bearing regional claim, retain:

- target jurisdiction, geography, and population;
- source role and language;
- data/event date and publication date;
- whether the evidence is direct, externally transferred, or contextual;
- the institutional, regulatory, demographic, economic, or cultural mismatch
  that could change interpretation.

When a current government URL has moved, follow `source-access.md`. Prefer the
current official document or catalog record and retain stable identifiers.
