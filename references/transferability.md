# Cross-context transferability

Use this reference when recommending that a policy, practice, product, program,
or research finding be adopted in a different region, population, institution,
market, or time period.

Evidence strength and transferability are separate judgments. Strong evidence
that something worked elsewhere does not establish that it will work here.

## Assess the transfer mechanism

Compare source and target contexts across:

- mechanism and intended outcome;
- population, baseline risk, and user behavior;
- law, governance, enforcement, and institutional incentives;
- infrastructure, staffing, cost, and implementation capacity;
- market structure and distribution channels;
- maturity, time horizon, and reversibility;
- plausible harms, displaced costs, and affected groups.

## Four transfer levels

1. **Directly transferable:** the causal mechanism and material prerequisites
   closely match, and target-context evidence does not reveal a major conflict.
2. **Transferable with adaptation:** the mechanism is relevant, but named legal,
   operational, population, or market changes are required.
3. **Pilot only:** transfer is plausible but important prerequisites or local
   effects remain uncertain. Recommend a bounded test with success and stop
   criteria rather than broad adoption.
4. **Not transferable:** direct counterevidence or a specific incompatible
   prerequisite makes the proposed transfer unlikely to work or unacceptably
   harmful in the target context.

Do not use “not transferable” merely because local evidence is absent. Absence
normally supports “pilot only” or unresolved. State what new evidence would
move the assessment to another level.

## Reporting

For each recommendation, show the source context, target context, evidence
strength, transfer level, required adaptation, and the main uncertainty. Keep
observed facts separate from the inference that transfer will succeed.
