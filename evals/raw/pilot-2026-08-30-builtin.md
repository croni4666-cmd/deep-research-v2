## Case A

### 1) Raw final answer

As of August 30, 2026, all four jurisdictions use semiconductor industrial policy, but with different centers of gravity:

- China emphasizes tax preferences, state-backed equity capital, technology self-reliance planning, and control over critical upstream materials.
- The United States combines large statutory grants and R&D spending with a 35% investment tax credit, government equity participation, export controls, and selective tariffs.
- Japan relies heavily on project-specific subsidies, a seven-year public-support commitment, production and R&D tax incentives, and economic-security supply guarantees.
- The EU operates chiefly through shared R&D infrastructure and Member State aid under EU rules. Its proposed Chips Act 2.0 would add stronger demand-side and strategic-project tools.

Amounts are not directly comparable: some are appropriations, others are tax expenditures, public-and-private investment totals, loans, guarantees, or project ceilings.

| Jurisdiction | Enacted or operational measures | Proposals or not-yet-operative measures |
|---|---|---|
| **China** | The 2020 State Council policy remains the tax-policy foundation: qualifying sub-28 nm production can receive ten years’ corporate-income-tax exemption; less advanced qualifying production gets shorter exemptions or reduced rates; design, equipment, materials, packaging and testing firms also qualify for preferences, alongside import-duty and import-VAT measures. China’s 2026 eligibility notice confirms these benefits remain operational and requires firms to reapply for the annual list. A separate measure permits an additional R&D deduction equal to 120% of qualifying expenditure through 2027. [State Council policy](https://www.miit.gov.cn/xwdt/szyw/art/2020/art_b5059afaa450422a820648c635d653f6.html), [2026 eligibility notice](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100013/c5248840/content.html), [R&D deduction](https://www.mof.gov.cn/jrttts/202309/t20230928_3909593.htm) China also uses state-backed equity: the financial regulator approved Bank of China’s ¥21.5 billion, 6.25% holding in the third National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund, implying ¥344 billion total subscribed capital. [NFRA approval](https://www.nfra.gov.cn/cn/view/pages/ItemDetail.html?docId=1165032&generaltype=1&itemId=4110) The National People’s Congress approved the 15th Five-Year Plan in March 2026; it calls for exceptional, whole-chain efforts in integrated circuits and expanded university capacity in the field. This is an adopted policy framework, although individual implementing projects still require later measures. [NPC resolution](https://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202603/t20260313_453198.html), [plan text](https://www.cppcc.gov.cn/zxww/2026/03/16/ARTI1773644951646391.shtml) Export licensing on gallium and germanium semiconductor materials remains part of the active system, as do April 2025 controls on seven medium and heavy rare-earth groups. [Gallium/germanium controls](https://exportcontrol.mofcom.gov.cn/article/zcfg/gnzcfg/zcfggzqd/202307/847.html), [2025 rare-earth controls](https://english.mofcom.gov.cn/Policies/AnnouncementsOrders/art/2025/art_0dd87cbee7b045bf93fabe6ab2faceee.html) | No discrete, pending central “semiconductor act” was identified in the reviewed primary sources. Also, the more expansive October 2025 rare-earth controls—covering some foreign-made items and semiconductor end uses—are **not currently operative**: announcements 61 and 62 are suspended until November 10, 2026. [MOFCOM suspension notice](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/cms_files/filemanager/policySummary/viewcore_dfb2fa8cb9d848d78eb63c666c8c605e.html) |
| **United States** | The CHIPS and Science Act remains the core: Commerce administers $39 billion in manufacturing incentives and $11 billion in semiconductor R&D, within $52.7 billion of semiconductor-related funding across agencies. The program continues issuing awards and funding opportunities in 2026. [NIST CHIPS overview](https://www.nist.gov/chips), [CHIPS funding fact sheet](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2024/04/22/Fact%20Sheet%20-%20Federal%20Incentives-updated-508C.pdf) The section 48D advanced-manufacturing investment credit rose from 25% to **35%** for qualifying semiconductor or semiconductor-equipment property placed in service after 2025. [IRS guidance](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/advanced-manufacturing-investment-credit) Award administration has shifted toward negotiated taxpayer upside and equity: the government converted $8.9 billion associated with Intel into a passive 9.9% stake, and July 2026 CHIPS R&D letters of intent require minority, non-controlling government stakes. [White House summary](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/WHOSTP-2025-Wins.pdf), [NIST July 2026 awards](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/07/department-commerce-announces-letters-intent-7-companies-874-million) A January 2026 Section 232 proclamation imposed an immediate 25% tariff on a narrow class of advanced computing chips when imports do not support specified U.S. uses or supply-chain buildout. Export licensing for H200-, MI325X- and comparable chips to China now proceeds case by case subject to capacity, customer-compliance and U.S. testing conditions. [Section 232 proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/01/adjusting-imports-of-semiconductors-semiconductor-manufacturing-equipment-and-their-derivative-products-into-the-united-states/), [BIS rule summary](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/department-commerce-revises-license-review-policy-semiconductors-exported-china) | The Section 232 proclamation’s proposed second phase—broader semiconductor tariffs plus an offset for companies investing in U.S. production—had not yet been imposed by the cited proclamation and should be treated as a plan. Two pending congressional examples are H.R. 802, which would extend the investment credit to semiconductor-design expenditures, and S. 1642, which would extend it to integral semiconductor materials. Both primary records show only introduction and committee referral. [H.R. 802 record](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-119hr802ih), [S. 1642 record](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-119s1642is) |
| **Japan** | Japan’s AI and Semiconductor Industry Foundation Strengthening Framework commits more than ¥10 trillion of public support through FY2030, intended to induce more than ¥50 trillion in public-private investment. [METI framework](https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/mono_info_service/ai_semiconductor_frame/ai_semiconductor_frame.html) A 2025 amendment, passed in April and effective in August 2025, authorizes capital contributions, in-kind contributions and debt guarantees for designated advanced-semiconductor production, with special-account bonds permitted from FY2025 through FY2030. [METI legal page](https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/mono_info_service/joho/laws/josokuho.html) Implementation is highly project-specific: approved support includes up to ¥732 billion for JASM’s second fab, up to ¥150 billion for Kioxia/Western Digital, a ¥100 billion government equity investment in Rapidus, and up to ¥60 billion for Sony image-sensor capacity. [METI project summary](https://www.meti.go.jp/english/policy/0704_001.pdf), [Rapidus investment](https://www.meti.go.jp/english/speeches/press_conferences/2026/0227001.html), [Sony supply plan](https://www.meti.go.jp/english/speeches/press_conferences/2026/0417001.html) Japan also has a production-volume tax credit for domestic microcontroller and analog-semiconductor output. Its 2026 tax changes created a separate strategic-technology R&D credit category covering semiconductors and communications. [Production tax-credit page](https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/economy/kyosoryoku_kyoka/senryaku_zeisei.html), [2026 tax reform](https://www.mof.go.jp/public_relations/finance/202604/202604c.html) | METI’s March 6, 2026 industrial-competitiveness amendment would enable immediate depreciation or a 7% credit for qualifying large domestic investments and add supply-chain finance mechanisms. The latest primary status located still described it as a bill submitted to the Diet, so it is classified here as proposed rather than enacted. [METI bill announcement](https://www.meti.go.jp/english/press/2026/0306_003.html), [METI July policy-status document](https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/policy_management/seisaku_hyoka/2026/07fy_seisakuhenohanei.pdf) |
| **European Union** | Regulation (EU) 2023/1781, in force since September 21, 2023, remains the operative European Chips Act. Its three pillars cover shared R&D and pilot-line capacity, a security-of-supply framework for first-of-a-kind facilities, and coordinated monitoring/crisis response. The EU budget provides up to €3.3 billion for Chips for Europe; the broader Act was expected to mobilize more than €43 billion, rather than supplying that entire sum from the EU budget. [European Chips Act overview](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/european-chips-act), [EU budget explanation](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/eu-budget/motion/focus/eu-budget-bolsters-europes-technological-leadership-european-chips-act_en) Member State aid is central: the approved IPCEI on Microelectronics and Communication Technologies permits up to €8.1 billion of aid from 14 states, expected to unlock €13.7 billion privately. [Commission IPCEI record](https://competition-policy.ec.europa.eu/state-aid/ipcei/approved-ipceis/microelectronics-value-chain_en) | The Commission proposed **Chips Act 2.0** on June 3, 2026. It would widen first-of-a-kind aid across the value chain, designate strategic projects for EU/member-state co-investment, create demand accelerators and innovation-procurement tools, connect demand from AI/cloud infrastructure, establish a business-to-business resilience platform, and recognize semiconductor regions of excellence. It is not enacted: Parliament records procedure 2026/0139(COD) in its preparatory phase. [Commission proposal](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/proposal-chips-act-20), [proposal measures](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/chips-act-2), [Parliament procedure status](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-file?reference=2026%2F0139%28COD%29) |

Bottom line: China’s system is the most state-directed and self-reliance-oriented; the U.S. now mixes CHIPS incentives with tax credits, equity, tariffs and export licensing; Japan concentrates very large support on selected supply-chain anchors; and the EU uses coordinated R&D plus controlled Member State aid. Only the EU has a comprehensive replacement act formally pending. The U.S. has narrower pending bills and a planned broader tariff phase; Japan has an enabling industrial-investment bill whose enacted status was not established by the latest primary record found; China’s adopted plan still requires project-level implementation.

## Case B

### 1) Raw final answer

The NQ-7 claim does **not** have independent affirmative support in the candidate set.

There are four publications, but only two evidentiary lineages:

1. **Asterion Energy press release** — the originating claim. It says the NQ-7 retained 96% capacity after 2,000 cycles and attributes the test to Northstar Validation Labs, but supplies no report, protocol, dataset, test conditions, sample count, report identifier, or registry entry.

2. **Industry Wire** — not independent. It expressly says the item was supplied by Asterion, published without editorial verification, and carries the same release identifier, AE-2026-0714.

3. **Market Digest** — not independent. It identifies the item as sponsored company news, says it was automatically generated from Asterion’s release, and says it was not separately fact-checked.

4. **Northstar public-registry search** — institutionally independent of the three publicity items and relevant to verification, but it does **not** support the claimed result. It found no public report matching Asterion/NQ-7 through August 1, 2026. The registry also explicitly warns that this absence does not prove a private test did not occur.

Thus, three apparent reports reduce to a single company assertion. Northstar supplies independent evidence only about the absence of a matching **public** report, not evidence that the claimed test result is true or false.

What remains unresolved:

- whether Northstar performed any private NQ-7 test;
- the report identifier and authorization to inspect it;
- cell format, chemistry and production lot;
- number of tested cells and treatment of failures;
- charge/discharge rate, depth of discharge and voltage limits;
- temperature and rest periods;
- the capacity measurement method and definition of “initial” capacity;
- raw cycle data, calibration records and uncertainty;
- whether 96% was representative, an average, or a selected specimen;
- whether Asterion influenced the protocol, analysis, or publication.

A defensible conclusion is: **“Unverified manufacturer claim; no independent affirmative corroboration in the candidate sources.”** Verification would require the laboratory report and underlying protocol/data, ideally followed by replication by a laboratory not retained by Asterion.
