## CASE MARKET — Global EV demand through 2030

### 1. Raw user-facing answer

Global electric-car demand is very likely to keep growing through 2030, but one precise forecast would be misleading. Electric cars—battery-electric plus plug-in hybrid in the cited forecasts—accounted for about 21 million sales and 25% of global car sales in 2025. For 2026, IEA and BloombergNEF independently expect roughly 23 million sales and a 27–28% share. [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/executive-summary), [BloombergNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-transport/bloombergnefs-electric-vehicle-outlook-2026-global-ev-sales-set-for-another-record-breaking-year-but-growth-in-some-major-markets-slows/)

For 2030, recent central estimates cluster around the high-30-million range: BloombergNEF’s 2026 outlook is reported at 35.4 million passenger EVs, or approximately 38% of registrations, while Counterpoint projects a 36% share. The IEA’s 2025 stated-policy forecast was somewhat higher at over 40% of global car sales. [BNEF figure reported by electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2026/06/17/bnef-forecast-global-electric-car-market-to-grow-by-eleven-per-cent-in-2026/), [Counterpoint](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/ev-outlook-revised-amid-oem-pullback-but-long-term-fundamentals-remain-strong), [IEA 2025](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2025/executive-summary)

Decision-relevant scenarios—my synthesis, not additional published forecasts—are:

| 2030 scenario | Global passenger EV sales | Approximate new-car share | What would produce it |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Downside | 30–34 million | 32–36% | Further US/EU policy retreat, trade barriers, weak charging rollout, persistent price premiums outside China |
| Central | 35–40 million | 38–43% | Current-policy momentum, continued battery-cost decline, China above half of world EV demand, moderate emerging-market adoption |
| Upside | 41–47 million | 44–50% | Faster price parity, high oil prices, stronger standards and incentives, rapid diffusion of affordable Chinese or local models |

Plan capacity against the central range, but stress-test economics at roughly 30 million and supply-chain/charging requirements near 45 million. Regional exposure matters more than the global total: China supplied about 60% of 2025 global EV sales, while US policy assumptions have caused unusually large forecast revisions.

Credible forecasts disagree because they differ in:

- Definition: passenger cars versus all light-duty vehicles; BEV-only versus BEV plus PHEV; “electric” versus zero-emission, which usually excludes PHEVs.
- Policy cutoff: “current policies,” “stated policies,” proposed rules, and announced targets produce different paths.
- Denominator: a sales-share forecast also depends on assumptions about total global vehicle sales.
- Technology and prices: battery costs, model availability, charging, financing, and resale assumptions vary.
- Regional weighting: China dominates the global result; modest changes there move the global forecast materially.
- Publication date: BNEF reduced its outlook twice following US policy rollback and a maturing Chinese market, illustrating genuine forecast instability.

Confidence is high that sales rise materially and reach at least roughly one-third of new cars; moderate on the 35–40 million central range; low on any exact 2030 point estimate.

## CASE MEDICAL — Routine screening for a hypothetical condition

### 1. Raw user-facing answer

Routine population screening for adults aged 40–55 is **not supportable from the information supplied**. The condition, jurisdiction, screening test, treatment pathway, prevalence, risk profile, and intended outcome are all unspecified. Those details determine both benefit and harm; inventing them would produce a false clinical recommendation.

The appropriate provisional decision is therefore: **do not introduce routine population screening until condition-specific evidence demonstrates positive net benefit**. This is not evidence that screening is ineffective; it is a conclusion that the evidence needed to assess it has not been defined.

Guideline frameworks are broadly consistent but emphasize different aspects:

- The US Preventive Services Task Force estimates benefit minus harm and separately grades the certainty of that estimate. Because screening is offered to asymptomatic people, many receive no benefit but remain exposed to immediate downstream harms. [USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/about-uspstf/methods-and-processes/procedure-manual/procedure-manual-section-6-methods-arriving-recommendation)
- The UK National Screening Committee requires a validated and acceptable test, understood natural history, an effective presymptomatic intervention, preferably high-quality randomized evidence for the complete pathway, benefits exceeding harms, and reasonable cost-effectiveness. [UK NSC criteria](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/evidence-review-criteria-national-screening-programmes/criteria-for-appraising-the-viability-effectiveness-and-appropriateness-of-a-screening-programme)
- WHO emphasizes that screening is a programme—not merely a test—and requires quality assurance, follow-up capacity, monitoring, and evidence that earlier detection improves outcomes. It warns that clear effectiveness evidence is often missing. [WHO](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789289054782)

Potential benefit would be reduced disease-specific morbidity, mortality, or complications because effective treatment starts earlier. Test sensitivity or stage shift alone is insufficient: lead-time bias, length bias, and overdiagnosis can make detection look beneficial without improving health.

Material harms include false positives, invasive confirmation, treatment complications, overdiagnosis and overtreatment, anxiety, false reassurance, incidental or uncertain findings, unequal access, and diversion of clinical resources. In a lower-prevalence general population, even a reasonably accurate test can yield many false positives.

Minimum evidence needed before recommending screening:

1. Age-specific prevalence and natural history in the intended jurisdiction.
2. A validated test and threshold, with sensitivity, specificity, and predictive values.
3. Evidence that treatment during the detectable presymptomatic phase improves patient-important outcomes.
4. Ideally randomized trials of invitation to screening versus usual care, measuring morbidity or mortality—not just detection.
5. Quantified false positives, overdiagnosis, procedure and treatment harms.
6. Implementation, equity, quality-assurance, and cost-effectiveness evidence.

Until those slots are filled, guideline comparison can only be methodological, not condition-specific. A named condition, country, test, and average-risk versus high-risk population would be required for a clinical recommendation.

## CASE ACADEMIC — Evaluation of retrieval-augmented generation

### 1. Raw user-facing answer

Recent peer-reviewed work converges on one conclusion: RAG cannot be evaluated reliably with a single answer-accuracy score. Evaluation must separate retrieval, generation, their interaction, and operational behavior.

**Datasets**

- Legacy open-domain QA collections—Natural Questions, TriviaQA, HotpotQA, KILT and related tasks—support comparable retrieval and answer evaluation, but are largely static, Wikipedia/web-centric, often dominated by single-hop factual questions, and may predate modern RAG.
- RGB is bilingual English/Chinese and stress-tests four abilities: noise robustness, negative rejection, information integration, and counterfactual robustness. Six evaluated LLMs still struggled particularly with rejection, integration, and false evidence. [AAAI RGB](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/29728)
- CRAG contains 4,409 question-answer pairs across five domains and eight categories, including long-tail entities and temporal dynamics from years to seconds. Straightforward RAG raised leading-model accuracy only to 44%, exposing the gap between benchmark QA and trustworthy dynamic retrieval. [NeurIPS CRAG](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/1435d2d0fca85a84d83ddcb754f58c29-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)
- Domain-specific and synthetic question–context–answer sets improve relevance to a deployment, but can inherit generator bias and fail to reflect real query frequency or failure patterns.

**Metrics**

- Retrieval: Precision@k, Recall@k, MRR and nDCG, supplemented by passage or claim coverage. Recall is especially important when missing one supporting fact invalidates a multi-part answer.
- Generation: exact match/F1 for constrained answers; answer correctness, relevance, faithfulness or groundedness, completeness, and citation precision/recall for open responses.
- End to end: robustness to irrelevant, missing, conflicting, or counterfactual evidence; calibrated abstention; and error attribution to retriever versus generator.
- Production: p95/p99 latency, time to first token, throughput, cost per query, index size, freshness, security, and drift.

RAGAS provides reference-free LLM-assisted measures across retrieval and generation dimensions. [EACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-demo.16/) ARES trains lightweight judges on synthetic data and calibrates aggregate estimates with a few hundred human labels through prediction-powered inference. [NAACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.20/) RAGChecker uses fine-grained retrieval and generation diagnostics and reported stronger correlation with human judgment than comparator metrics. [NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/27245589131d17368cccdfa990cbf16e-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)

**Methodological limitations**

- End-answer scores conflate retrieval failure with failure to use good evidence.
- Faithfulness and correctness differ: an answer can faithfully repeat a false retrieved document.
- Lexical metrics poorly capture long-form semantic quality; LLM judges improve flexibility but introduce prompt/model dependence, nondeterminism, cost, and potential shared-model bias.
- Gold answers and gold passages are expensive and often incomplete; synthetic labels can reproduce the same model’s preferences.
- Public benchmarks are narrow relative to production domains, languages, temporal change, multi-turn use, citation requirements, and adversarial retrieval.
- Refusal metrics can reward a system that refuses too often unless accuracy is evaluated jointly with coverage.
- Many papers under-report latency, cost, privacy, poisoning, prompt injection, and longitudinal drift.
- A 2026 systematic review found the evidence base still small and partly dependent on gray literature; its search was conducted in August 2024, limiting recency despite the later journal publication. [SN Computer Science](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42979-026-05134-x)

A defensible evaluation design should therefore combine: a frozen public benchmark; a representative, time-stamped domain test set; gold retrieval and answer annotations on a stratified subset; separate retrieval and generation diagnostics; blinded human review; a calibrated LLM judge; robustness/abstention tests; and operational/security measurements. Report confidence intervals and preserve queries, corpus snapshots, prompts, judge versions, and random seeds.

## CASE TECHNICAL — Python workflow orchestration for a regulated pipeline

### 1. Raw user-facing answer

Assumption: the comparison set is Apache Airflow, Dagster, and Prefect, and “regulated” means that reproducibility, separation of duties, audit trails, vulnerability response, lineage, and contractual support matter.

Current stable PyPI releases verified on 30 August 2026:

| Library | Stable release | Released | Maintenance/support assessment |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Apache Airflow | 3.3.1 | 12 Aug 2026 | Major version 3 is in maintenance; version 2 reached EOL on 22 Apr 2026 |
| Dagster | 1.13.20 | 27 Aug 2026 | Active weekly releases; officially remains supported following Prefect’s July 2026 acquisition announcement |
| Prefect | 3.8.4 | 25 Aug 2026 | Active; 3.8.5.dev builds are prereleases and therefore excluded |

Version evidence: [Airflow PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/apache-airflow/), [Dagster PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/dagster/), [Prefect PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/prefect/).

**Airflow** is the strongest default for a self-managed, heavily governed environment. It has mature operational deployment patterns, provider breadth, structured audit logs, role separation, official constraints for reproducible installation, an Apache governance model, and an explicit lifecycle. However, security fixes are issued only for the latest minor line, so staying patched requires regular minor upgrades. The project also states that its volunteer-driven process does not guarantee eliminating every third-party dependency CVE, DAG authors are trusted code executors, and only Linux production deployments are officially supported. [Lifecycle](https://airflow.apache.org/docs/apache-airflow/stable/installation/supported-versions.html), [security patches](https://airflow.apache.org/docs/apache-airflow/stable/security/releasing_security_patches.html), [audit logs](https://airflow.apache.org/docs/apache-airflow/stable/security/audit_logs.html), [security model](https://airflow.apache.org/docs/apache-airflow/stable/security/security_model.html)

**Dagster** is strongest when data-asset lineage, testability, partitioning, freshness, and observable transformations are central to the control design. Dagster+ uses a hybrid architecture in which customer code and data remain in the customer environment; the vendor claims SOC 2 Type II and HIPAA support. Audit logs, SIEM export, premium support, and uptime SLAs appear in the higher commercial tier rather than as universal open-source guarantees. Prefect’s acquisition of Dagster Labs adds vendor-concentration and roadmap risk, although the joint announcement states that the product, name, license, contracts, pricing, and support continue. [Security](https://dagster.io/security), [commercial feature matrix](https://dagster.io/pricing), [acquisition/support statement](https://dagster.io/prefect)

**Prefect** offers the lightest transition from ordinary Python and a flexible hybrid execution model. Its Enterprise service claims SOC 2 Type II, SSO, granular RBAC, audit logging, PrivateLink-style connectivity, a 99.99% uptime SLA, and a 30-minute P1 response on the specified Gold tier. Those are paid-service claims requiring contractual confirmation. The self-hosted server explicitly does not include all Cloud organizational features such as RBAC, audit logs, and SSO. Prefect documents a minimum deprecation period of three minor versions or six months, but also warns that newer clients can be incompatible with older servers. [Enterprise](https://www.prefect.io/enterprise), [self-hosted distinction](https://docs.prefect.io/v3/concepts/server), [versioning policy](https://docs.prefect.io/latest/contributing/versioning), [Cloud audit logs](https://docs.prefect.io/v3/how-to-guides/cloud/manage-users/audit-logs)

Recommendation:

- Choose **Airflow 3.3.1** when open-source governance, ecosystem depth, and self-managed auditability outweigh upgrade and operational complexity.
- Choose **Dagster 1.13.20/Dagster+** when asset lineage and data-quality controls are the primary compliance evidence, subject to acquisition-roadmap and contract review.
- Choose **Prefect 3.8.4 Enterprise** when Python ergonomics, hybrid execution, and a contractual SaaS support posture matter most.
- Do not treat SOC 2, HIPAA readiness, audit logs, or an SLA as proof that the pipeline itself is compliant. Require an architecture review, data-flow inventory, access-control test, retention policy, disaster-recovery evidence, vendor report/contract review, locked dependencies, SBOM and vulnerability process, validated upgrade procedure, and immutable export of logs to the organization’s own retention system.

No public official LTS commitment comparable to a multi-year enterprise Linux support window was verified for Dagster or Prefect. For Airflow, only the latest minor receives normal patch/security releases. That lifecycle fact may be more important to a regulated buyer than feature comparisons.
