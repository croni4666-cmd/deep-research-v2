**Bottom line.** All four jurisdictions now use public finance to reshape semiconductor supply chains, but the instruments differ. China emphasizes full-chain technological self-reliance through planning, tax preferences, and state-backed equity; the U.S. couples large manufacturing and R&D incentives with guardrails, export controls, and tariffs; Japan concentrates support on named strategic projects and supply-security plans; and the EU combines common research infrastructure with coordinated national state aid. The headline figures are not directly comparable: Japan’s ¥10 trillion includes AI; the EU’s €43 billion is expected aggregate public investment; the U.S. $50 billion is the Commerce-administered CHIPS portion; and China does not publish a single consolidated national semiconductor-support total in the inspected sources.

Material uncertainty remains around unpublished Chinese fund allocations and local subsidies, future Japanese annual appropriations, final U.S. letters of intent and second-phase tariffs, and the final negotiated text and budget treatment of EU Chips Act 2.0.

## Case B

### 1) Raw final answer

**Conclusion: the NQ-7 cycle-life claim does not have independent positive support in the candidate set.**

The three sources reporting “96% capacity after 2,000 cycles” form **one evidence group**, not three:

- Asterion’s press release is the originating first-party assertion.
- Industry Wire says the item was supplied by Asterion, carries the same release identifier, and was published without editorial verification.
- Market Digest labels its item sponsored company news, identifies Asterion release AE-2026-0714 as the source, and says the summary was automatically generated and not separately fact-checked.

Different publishers and URLs therefore do not create independent corroboration. All three depend on the same underlying company release and the same unproduced alleged Northstar test.

Northstar’s registry result is a **second, independent provenance group**, but it does not support the performance claim. It establishes only that no public report matching Asterion, NQ-7, and the specified date range appeared in the searched registry as of 1 August 2026. The registry explicitly warns that absence does not prove a private test did not occur.

A defensible evidence ledger would therefore classify the central claim as **unresolved**, or at most **qualified as “reported by Asterion”**:

- “Asterion publicly claimed 96% after 2,000 cycles” is verified by the release.
- “The NQ-7 actually achieved 96% after 2,000 cycles” has only one underlying source group and no inspectable test evidence.
- “Northstar independently verified the result” is unsupported because no Northstar report, protocol, data, or signed statement is present.

Still unresolved are the report identifier; whether Northstar performed the test; laboratory identity and accreditation; cell or pack configuration; sample size; cycle definition; charge/discharge rate; depth of discharge; temperature; rest periods; capacity-measurement method; pass/fail criterion; raw data; calibration and chain of custody; statistical variability; sponsor control; and authorization to inspect the result.

**Stopping judgment:** further comparison among the three derivative stories would add no evidence because their provenance is already explicit. The conclusion could change only if the underlying Northstar report, protocol and dataset—or another genuinely independent test—became available.
