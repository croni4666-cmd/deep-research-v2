## Case A

### 1) Raw final answer

As of 2026-08-30, all four jurisdictions use semiconductor policy to build domestic capacity and reduce supply-chain risk, but their instruments differ:

| Jurisdiction | Enacted/current measures | Proposals or non-final measures |
|---|---|---|
| China | The broadest state-directed stack: node-specific corporate-tax holidays; duty exemptions and deferred import VAT for qualifying producers and projects; a 120% additional R&D-expense deduction through 2027; government-guided investment funds; and national planning that prioritizes full-chain integrated-circuit breakthroughs. China also controls exports of strategically relevant inputs such as gallium and germanium. | The 15th Five-Year Plan itself is enacted, not merely proposed. Its semiconductor objectives are binding policy direction, but many project-level budgets, implementing rules, and award decisions remain unspecified. I found no separate national draft semiconductor statute comparable to the EU’s Chips Act 2.0 in the inspected official sources. |
| United States | The CHIPS and Science Act supplies Commerce with $50 billion—$39 billion for manufacturing incentives and $11 billion for R&D. The Section 48D investment credit is 35% for qualifying semiconductor manufacturing property placed in service from 2026. Commerce continues executing definitive awards and funding competitions. Security policy includes export licensing for advanced chips and equipment, plus a January 2026 Section 232 tariff of 25% on a narrow class of advanced-computing chips when imports do not support specified U.S. supply-chain uses. | July 2026 letters of intent covering up to $874 million of semiconductor R&D remain subject to diligence and final approval. The administration’s second tariff phase—broader semiconductor tariffs at a “significant” rate, accompanied by a tariff-offset mechanism for U.S. investors—was announced but not yet imposed in the inspected proclamation. |
| Japan | Japan combines project-specific subsidies with economic-security supply plans. Official records show up to ¥732 billion for JASM’s second fab and an additional ¥802.5 billion decided for Rapidus in FY2025. A 2025 legal amendment, in force since August 2025, created capital-contribution, in-kind contribution, debt-guarantee, and dedicated-account mechanisms for advanced semiconductors and AI, underpinning a framework of more than ¥10 trillion in public support through FY2030. Japan also subjects 23 categories of advanced semiconductor-manufacturing equipment to export control. | METI’s January 2026 statement said it would “promptly examine” broader capital-investment and R&D support and described intended tax incentives. Those statements are policy intentions unless and until reflected in enacted budgets or tax law. The ¥100 billion Rapidus investment moved beyond a generic proposal when METI selected Rapidus in November 2025 and identified an FY2025 budget allocation, although actual disbursement should not be inferred from selection alone. |
| European Union | Regulation (EU) 2023/1781 remains in force. It establishes the Chips for Europe capacity-building initiative, a framework for first-of-a-kind production facilities, and supply monitoring/crisis coordination. The EU budget contributes up to €3.3 billion; the Commission describes more than €43 billion of policy-driven investment through 2030, including national and private components—not €43 billion of EU spending. | The Commission’s Chips Act 2.0 proposal of 3 June 2026 is not enacted. It would add stronger industrial-deployment and demand-side tools, including strategic projects, grand challenges, a demand forum and accelerators, photonic-chip capacity, faster permitting, procurement mechanisms, and “Semiconductor Regions of Excellence.” It remains subject to the EU legislative process and future budget decisions. |

The clearest comparison is:

- **China:** most directive and vertically integrated, using tax selection, state-guided capital, national technology missions, and materials controls.
- **United States:** largest explicit federal manufacturing-and-R&D appropriation, reinforced by a large tax credit, transaction-specific awards, export controls, and tariffs.
- **Japan:** concentrated, project-led support aimed at restoring advanced logic capacity and protecting strengths in equipment and materials.
- **EU:** coordinated supranational capacity building plus member-state aid, with the proposed Chips Act 2.0 shifting toward demand aggregation, faster industrial deployment, and resilience.

Important qualification: enacted authorization, an appropriated envelope, a selected recipient, a signed final award, and cash actually disbursed are different stages. The figures above should not be added or compared as if they represented equivalent realized spending.

## Case B

### 1) Raw final answer

The NQ-7 claim does **not** have independent affirmative support in the four-source fixture.

The three favorable publications form only one evidence group:

1. Asterion Energy’s press release is the original first-party assertion.
2. Industry Wire explicitly says its item was supplied by Asterion and published without editorial verification. It reproduces the same release identifier, AE-2026-0714.
3. Market Digest says its sponsored summary was automatically generated from that same release and was not separately fact-checked.

Different publishers and URLs therefore do not create independent evidence. All three depend on Asterion’s single underlying statement that NQ-7 retained 96% capacity after 2,000 cycles.

The Northstar registry is genuinely independent of those copies as a source, but it does not confirm the performance result. Its search found no public report matching Asterion and NQ-7 through 2026-08-01. That absence weakens public verifiability but does not prove that a private test never occurred.

Accordingly:

- **Supported:** Asterion publicly made the claim, and two outlets reproduced it.
- **Not independently supported:** The asserted 96%-after-2,000-cycles result itself.
- **Not contradicted:** The registry result is absence of public evidence, not evidence of test failure or fabrication.
- **Unresolved:** Whether Northstar performed a private test; the report identifier; test protocol; cell count and sampling; temperature; charge/discharge rates and depth of discharge; definition of a completed cycle; initial-capacity baseline; failure criteria; raw dataset; calibration and chain of custody; and whether Northstar authorized Asterion’s characterization of the result as “independent.”

The minimum evidence needed to upgrade the claim would be an authenticated Northstar report or registry entry with protocol and results. Stronger support would include inspectable data and replication by a second laboratory using an independently designed or preregistered protocol.
